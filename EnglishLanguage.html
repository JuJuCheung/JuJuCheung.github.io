AQA PAPER 1

Paper 1 Practice

📖 Fiction Extract

Read the following extract carefully before answering the questions.

The road disappeared into the forest. Maya stopped at the edge of the trees and looked behind her. The small village was already hidden by the hill. For the first time, she realised just how far she had walked. Above her, branches twisted together and blocked most of the afternoon sunlight. The bright summer day seemed to vanish as soon as she stepped under the trees. She continued along the narrow path. At first, the forest seemed peaceful. Birds called from somewhere high above her and leaves moved gently in the breeze. But after several minutes, Maya noticed something strange. The birds had stopped. She stopped too. Nothing moved. Then she heard it. A sharp crack came from somewhere behind her. Maya turned quickly, but the path was empty. "Hello?" she called. There was no answer. She told herself that it was only a branch falling. Still, she began walking faster. The path curved around a group of enormous trees. Their dark trunks stood like silent guards on either side of her. Another crack. Closer this time. Maya started to run.

Paper 1 – Question 1

4 marks

Which statement about Maya is correct?

✅ Correct. The village is hidden by the hill, showing that Maya has walked some distance.

Paper 1 – Question 2: Language

8 marks

How does the writer use language to make the forest seem threatening?

Q2 Self-check
  • Have you used quotations?
  • Have you analysed important words?
  • Have you identified a language technique where useful?
  • Have you explained the effect on the reader?
Model Answer:

The writer makes the forest seem threatening through the description of the trees. The branches are described as being "twisted together". The word "twisted" suggests that the trees are unnatural and tangled, making the forest seem difficult to escape from. The writer also uses the simile "like silent guards" to describe the tree trunks. This makes the trees seem almost human, as if they are watching Maya and preventing her from leaving. The adjective "silent" also creates an uncomfortable atmosphere because the forest has become strangely quiet.

Paper 1 – Question 3: Structure

8 marks

How has the writer structured the text to interest you as a reader?

Q3 Self-check
  • Have you discussed the beginning, middle or ending?
  • Have you explained changes in focus?
  • Have you considered sentence length or pacing?
  • Have you explained why the structural choices interest the reader?
Model Answer:

At the beginning, the writer focuses on Maya entering the forest and establishes the isolated setting. The village disappearing behind the hill makes the reader realise that Maya is becoming increasingly alone. The focus then changes from the wider forest to small sounds and details. The writer tells us that "The birds had stopped", followed by the very short sentence "Nothing moved." This slows down the action and makes the silence more noticeable. Towards the end, the pace becomes faster. The repeated cracking sounds become closer to Maya, and the extract finishes with "Maya started to run." Ending at this moment creates suspense because the reader does not yet know what is following her.

Paper 1 – Question 4: Evaluation

20 marks

A student said: "The writer successfully makes Maya seem increasingly frightened as the extract develops."

To what extent do you agree?

Q4 Self-check
  • Have you clearly said how far you agree?
  • Have you used evidence?
  • Have you analysed language or structure?
  • Have you evaluated how effective the writer is?
Model Answer:

I strongly agree that the writer makes Maya seem increasingly frightened. At first, Maya appears reasonably calm as she walks through the forest. However, the mood begins to change when "The birds had stopped." The sudden silence makes the setting feel unnatural and alerts both Maya and the reader that something may be wrong. The writer then introduces the repeated sound of a "sharp crack". Repeating this sound makes the danger seem as if it is moving closer to Maya. The short sentences also reflect her growing fear: "She stopped too. Nothing moved." The short structure slows the reader down and increases suspense. Finally, the writer ends with "Maya started to run." This is effective because Maya has changed from walking calmly to escaping from something she believes may be following her. Therefore, the writer successfully shows her fear increasing throughout the extract.

AQA PAPER 2

Paper 2 Practice

📄 Source A – Modern Article

Last summer, I decided to spend a week without my phone. On the first morning, I reached for it before I had even opened my eyes. Then I remembered that it was locked in a drawer downstairs. At first, the silence felt uncomfortable. I normally checked messages, videos and news before breakfast. Without the screen, the morning seemed strangely long. By the third day, however, something had changed. I noticed how much time I usually wasted scrolling. I began walking more, reading books and talking to people without checking notifications every few minutes. The experiment did not make me hate technology. In fact, I still think phones are incredibly useful. But it showed me that sometimes we need to switch them off before they start controlling our attention.

📜 Source B – Older Account

During my childhood, evenings were rarely spent indoors. After supper, the children of the neighbourhood gathered outside. We invented games, explored the fields and stayed out until the street lamps appeared. There were no glowing screens demanding our attention and no machines buzzing in our pockets. Entertainment depended upon imagination and company. Of course, life was not perfect. Rain could ruin our plans and winter evenings were often dull. Yet I remember those simple hours with great affection. We had fewer forms of entertainment, perhaps, but we were rarely short of things to do.

Paper 2 – Question 1

4 marks

Which statement about Source A is correct?

✅ Correct. Source A says the writer deliberately spent one week without their phone.

Paper 2 – Question 2: Summary / Comparison

8 marks

Use details from both sources. Write a summary of the differences between the writers' experiences of spending their free time.

Q2 Self-check
  • Have you used information from Source A?
  • Have you used information from Source B?
  • Have you identified differences?
  • Have you made an inference about those differences?
Model Answer:

The two writers have different experiences of spending their free time without technology. In Source A, being without a phone initially feels unusual and uncomfortable. The writer says that "the morning seemed strangely long", suggesting that they are used to filling their free time by checking their phone. In contrast, the writer in Source B remembers naturally spending free time outdoors with other children. They "invented games" and "explored the fields", suggesting that they were used to creating their own entertainment. Therefore, Source A presents being without technology as something the writer has to learn to enjoy, whereas in Source B it was a normal part of everyday childhood.

Paper 2 – Question 3: Language

12 marks

How does the writer of Source B use language to describe life without modern technology?

Q3 Self-check
  • Have you selected quotations from Source B?
  • Have you analysed individual words?
  • Have you identified useful techniques?
  • Have you explained the effects?
Model Answer:

The writer presents childhood without modern technology in a positive and nostalgic way. The children "invented games" and "explored the fields". The verbs "invented" and "explored" suggest creativity and adventure, making their childhood seem active and exciting. The writer also describes "glowing screens demanding our attention". The verb "demanding" personifies modern technology as something forceful that constantly asks people to pay attention to it. In contrast, the writer says that entertainment depended on "imagination and company". These positive nouns emphasise creativity and friendship, suggesting that the writer values the simpler childhood they experienced.

Paper 2 – Question 4: Compare Viewpoints

16 marks

Compare how the writers convey their different attitudes towards technology and life without it.

Q4 Self-check
  • Have you compared both writers?
  • Have you explained each writer's viewpoint?
  • Have you used quotations from both sources?
  • Have you compared methods as well as ideas?
Model Answer:

Both writers recognise benefits to spending time without technology, but their viewpoints are different. The writer of Source A has a balanced attitude towards technology. They admit that phones are "incredibly useful", showing that they are not completely against them. However, the writer warns that people sometimes need to switch phones off before they begin "controlling our attention". This personification suggests that phones can gain too much influence over people's lives. In contrast, the writer of Source B is much more nostalgic. They remember childhood evenings with "great affection". The phrase shows the writer's warm emotional connection to a childhood without modern screens. Source B also contrasts modern technology with "imagination and company", suggesting that friendship and creativity were more important forms of entertainment. Therefore, Source A argues mainly for balance when using technology, whereas Source B looks back positively on a time when modern technology was not central to everyday life.
