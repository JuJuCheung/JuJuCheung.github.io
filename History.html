Year 11 History

Explore four major areas of history. Study how crime and punishment changed, how Elizabeth I ruled England, why the Cold War developed, and how medicine changed from the medieval period to the modern world.

History Topics

⚖️ Crime & Punishment

Explore how crime, policing and punishment changed over hundreds of years. Study medieval law, the Bloody Code, transportation, prisons, professional policing and modern approaches to punishment.

👑 Elizabethan England

Study the reign of Elizabeth I, including religion, Mary Queen of Scots, Catholic threats, the Spanish Armada, poverty, exploration and Elizabethan society.

☢️ The Cold War

Explore the rivalry between the USA and the Soviet Union after World War II. Study Berlin, the Cuban Missile Crisis, Vietnam, détente, Afghanistan and the collapse of the Soviet Union.

🩺 Medicine Through Time

Discover how medicine developed from medieval ideas to modern science. Study Galen, Vesalius, Harvey, Pasteur, Lister, vaccination, antibiotics and public health.

History Timeline

1348 – Black Death reaches England

The plague killed a large proportion of the population and exposed the limits of medieval medicine.

1543 – Vesalius publishes his anatomical studies

Vesalius used human dissection to challenge some of Galen's ideas.

1558 – Elizabeth I becomes Queen

Elizabeth inherited a country divided by religious and political tensions.

1559 – Religious Settlement

Elizabeth established a Protestant Church of England under royal authority.

1587 – Mary Queen of Scots executed

Mary was executed after being linked to plots against Elizabeth.

1588 – Spanish Armada

Philip II's invasion attempt was defeated by a combination of English resistance, Spanish problems and weather.

1829 – Metropolitan Police established

Robert Peel's Metropolitan Police Act created a professional London police force.

1861 – Pasteur and Germ Theory

Louis Pasteur demonstrated that microorganisms were involved in decay and disease.

1867 – Lister and antiseptic surgery

Joseph Lister used antiseptic techniques to reduce infections during surgery.

1948–49 – Berlin Blockade

The Soviet Union blocked Western access to West Berlin, leading to the Berlin Airlift.

1962 – Cuban Missile Crisis

The USA and USSR came extremely close to nuclear conflict.

1989 – Berlin Wall falls

The collapse of communist governments in Eastern Europe transformed the Cold War.

1991 – Soviet Union collapses

The dissolution of the USSR marked the end of the Cold War era.

Historical Interpretations

⚖️ Why did punishment change?

Punishment did not simply become more humane over time. Government, religion, social attitudes, technology, crime levels and ideas about rehabilitation all influenced change.

👑 Was Elizabeth successful?

Historians debate how far Elizabeth's success came from her own political skill and how far it depended on advisers, Parliament, local government and the weakness of her enemies.

☢️ Who caused the Cold War?

Historians disagree about responsibility. Some emphasise Soviet expansion, others American economic and strategic policy, while post-revisionist historians examine fear, ideology and misunderstandings on both sides.

🩺 Why did medicine improve?

Medical progress depended on more than individual scientists. Technology, war, government, communication, scientific knowledge and public attitudes all contributed to change.

“History is not simply remembering facts. It is explaining change, evaluating evidence and judging different interpretations.”
