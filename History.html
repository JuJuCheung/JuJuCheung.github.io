About the French Revolution

The French Revolution began in 1789. People in France challenged King Louis XVI and the unfair social structure known as the Ancien Régime.

High taxes, food shortages, rising bread prices, government debt and Enlightenment ideas all contributed to demands for change.

France abolished the monarchy and established a republic. However, the Revolution also led to political violence and the Reign of Terror.

Napoleon Bonaparte seized power in 1799 and became Emperor in 1804. He introduced reforms that changed French law, government and education.

Napoleon also fought wars across Europe. His rule ended following his defeat at the Battle of Waterloo in 1815.

Big Historical Question: Did Napoleon protect the achievements of the French Revolution, or betray its ideals?

Key History Topics

1780s–1789

🏛️ Causes of the Revolution

French society was divided into three estates.

The First Estate was the clergy. The Second Estate was the nobility. The Third Estate included peasants, workers and the middle classes.

Unequal taxes, financial problems, food shortages and Enlightenment ideas helped cause the Revolution.

1789–1791

📜 Revolution and Rights

The Third Estate formed the National Assembly and promised to create a constitution during the Tennis Court Oath.

On 14 July 1789, Parisians stormed the Bastille.

The Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen promoted liberty and equality before the law.

1792–1794

⚖️ Reign of Terror

France became a republic in 1792. King Louis XVI was executed in 1793.

Revolutionary leaders believed harsh measures were necessary to protect France from enemies.

Robespierre became one of the leading figures associated with the Terror. Thousands were executed.

1795–1799

🗳️ The Directory

After the Reign of Terror, France was governed by the Directory.

Its government faced corruption, economic problems and political instability.

Napoleon became popular through his military victories. He seized power in November 1799.

1799–1815

👑 Napoleon Bonaparte

Napoleon became First Consul in 1799 and Emperor of the French in 1804.

He introduced the Napoleonic Code, strengthened the administration and reorganised education.

However, he restricted political freedoms, controlled the press and restored slavery in French colonies.

1803–1815

⚔️ Napoleonic Wars

Napoleon fought major European powers, including Britain, Austria, Prussia and Russia.

He won the Battle of Austerlitz in 1805, but suffered major losses during the Russian campaign in 1812.

In 1815, Napoleon was defeated at Waterloo and exiled to Saint Helena.

Historical Interpretations

Was the Revolution inevitable?

Some historians focus on economic inequality and Enlightenment ideas. Others emphasise political mistakes, food shortages and immediate events.

Did the Revolution create freedom?

The Revolution challenged inherited privilege, but political and social inequalities remained. Women and many poorer citizens still lacked equal political rights.

Did Napoleon betray the Revolution?

Napoleon preserved legal reforms but concentrated power in his own hands. His rule combined revolutionary reforms with authoritarian government.

Why did Napoleon fall?

Military overexpansion, economic warfare, resistance in Spain, the failed Russian campaign and powerful coalitions contributed to Napoleon's defeat.
